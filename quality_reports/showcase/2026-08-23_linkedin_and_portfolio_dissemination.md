52: 🚀 How does Artificial Intelligence interact with large informal labor markets?
53: 
54: New research paper and open-source replication package:
55: 📄 "AI Exposure in an Informal Labor Market: Theory and Evidence from Brazil"
56: 
57: While the global AI literature predominantly focuses on advanced economies with low informality, emerging markets face a unique dual structure: high cognitive task content concentrated in formal firms alongside a massive informal sector (nearly 40% in Brazil).
58: 
59: Using an occupational assignment model with institutional frictions and microdata from IBGE's PNAD Contínua (2026Q1, N = 227,629 individuals representing 102.1 million workers) crosswalked to O*NET-SOC AI exposure scores via COD-ISCO, we document three primary empirical findings:
60: 
61: 1️⃣ Steep Educational Gradient (β̂₁ = 0.23, p < 0.001):
62: Each additional year of schooling increases occupational AI exposure by 0.23 points (0.10 SD), robust to full Mincerian controls and habitual income.
63: 
64: 2️⃣ Direct Formal Governance Channel (62.8%):
65: Exposed occupations exhibit 6.23 pp lower informality. Controlling for education, 62.8% of this gap persists—driven directly by formal compliance requirements and complementary organizational capital.
66: 
67: 3️⃣ Regional Polarization & "Informal Shield":
68: In states with higher formal sector maturity, the skill-sorting gradient is strictly steeper, with a critical tipping point at e* = 11.14 years of schooling. Informal employment offers temporary mechanical insulation from automation at the cost of a low-productivity trap.
